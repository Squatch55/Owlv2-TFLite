# Raising the Pass Rate of a Negative-Skew Prop-Firm Bot: A Goal-Reaching Control Approach

## TL;DR
- **Your problem is not accuracy and not drawdown — it is profit velocity under a deadline.** Goal-reaching/deadline control theory (Browne 1999; Kulldorff 1993; Pestien-Sudderth 1985) proves the optimal policy is *state-dependent and time-dependent*: be more aggressive the further you are below the $1,250 target and the less time remains, and de-risk as you approach the target or the trailing floor. Your "sprint-with-cushion vs floor-defense" intuition is exactly this theory's prediction, and constant-risk rules are provably suboptimal near a deadline.
- **The single highest-value lever is left-tail surgery that preserves density**, not higher win rate. Cap the worst losses (top 5 = 58.1% of gross loss, ~88% of net) via volatility-scaled stop *ceilings* and a dedicated loss-tail model (your fast-target AUC≈0.81 head, not the near-random win head), plus an event/time-of-day risk throttle on the MNQ 11:00–12:40 ET window — without the blanket suppression (K200) that cut pass30 from 30.8% to 17.9%.
- **No design can guarantee a 90% pass rate.** Intraday signal-to-noise is low, your edge is fragile, and the harsh NinjaTrader fill model alone collapses pass30/pass60 from 30.8%/56.4% to ~10.3%/12.8%. Every change must clear Deflated-Sharpe / PBO multiple-testing tests and regime-aware block-bootstrap (not day-shuffle) validation, under realistic fills and slippage, before risking capital.

## Key Findings

1. **The challenge is formally a goal-reaching-by-deadline problem with an upper absorbing barrier (the $1,250 target) and a moving lower barrier (the trailing floor).** The academic literature on this exact structure (Browne, Dubins-Savage, Pestien-Sudderth, Kulldorff) gives a precise, implementable prescription for *how risk should vary with floor gap, days remaining, distance to target, the $450 cap, and consistency headroom*. This is the organizing theory and the most novel contribution below.

2. **Your failure mode (timeout, regime-concentrated) means the binding constraint is drift, not variance.** Pestien-Sudderth (1985) proves that to maximize the probability of reaching a goal you maximize the drift-to-variance ratio μ/σ², and in the favorable case the log-optimal (Kelly) bet *minimizes expected time to the goal*. [Ebscohost](http://connection.ebscohost.com/c/articles/6981526/continuous-time-red-black-how-control-diffusion-goal) This is why blanket risk suppression (K200: maxDD $732→$358 but pass30 30.8%→17.9%) backfires — it cut σ but cut μ more, lengthening time-to-goal in exactly the weak regimes that already time out.

3. **The payoff asymmetry (avg loss −$142 vs avg win +$52, loss/win 2.71x) is a classic negative-skew "short-volatility-like" profile**, and the literature warns it is precisely the profile where tightening stops can destroy the rare large winners a high-WR system depends on. The fix must be *targeted* tail control (MAE-based ceilings + a tail model) rather than uniform stop-tightening.

4. **A complementary positive-skew momentum sleeve is theoretically the cleanest skew fix** and is consistent with your confirmed momentum-exhaustion hybrid result (pass60 56.4%→76.9%, timeouts 17/39→9/39). Baltussen, Da, Lammers & Martens (2021, *Journal of Financial Economics* 142:377–403) document robust market intraday momentum across 60+ futures (1974–2020), "translating into high and attractive (annualized) Sharpe ratios between 0.87 and 1.73 at the asset class level," where "the return during the last 30 minutes before the market close is positively predicted by the return during the rest of the day." This peer-reviewed result is a far stronger anchor than the retail ORB literature (Zarattini et al.), which is practitioner-grade with idealized fills.

5. **The right ML reframing is distribution-aware tail prediction, not classification.** Quantile regression for MAE/MFE, conformal prediction (Angelopoulos-Bates; Vovk) for distribution-free tail gating and abstention, calibration (isotonic/Platt) as a prerequisite for probability-driven sizing, and quickest-change-detection (CUSUM/Shiryaev-Roberts) for the regime cliff are all well-supported; distributional/CVaR RL is promising but reproducibility-fragile and should be last.

6. **Your validation needs Deflated Sharpe Ratio and Probability of Backtest Overfitting** (Bailey-Lopez de Prado), because selecting the best pass30 from dozens of candidates over ~39 starts with a hard regime split is a textbook multiple-testing trap. Day-shuffle Monte Carlo is over-optimistic because it destroys the regime clustering that drives your timeouts; regime-aware block bootstrap and CPCV are required.

## Details

### A. The organizing theory: reach $1,250 before day 30 without breaching a trailing floor

Frame the account state at any moment as a vector: current profit P (distance to target G = effective target = max($1,250, best_day/0.40)), the floor gap F (current equity minus trailing floor), days remaining D, intraday daily-cap headroom (≤$450) and daily-loss budget (≈$200), and consistency headroom (best-day constraint). The objective is to **maximize the probability of hitting G before day 30 (and 60) without F ever reaching zero.** This is a goal-reaching problem with an *upper absorbing barrier* (G, success) and a *moving lower barrier* (the trailing floor, failure) — a two-barrier, finite-horizon stochastic control problem.

**Pestien & Sudderth (1985, *Mathematics of Operations Research* 10(4):599–611, "Continuous-time red and black: how to control a diffusion to a goal").** To maximize the probability of reaching a goal, the controller should at each instant *maximize the drift-to-variance ratio μ/σ²*. In the subfair case (negative expectation) bold play is optimal; in the superfair case (positive expectation) timid proportional (Kelly-like) play is optimal, and the log-drift-maximizing strategy *minimizes the expected time to the goal*. [Ebscohost](http://connection.ebscohost.com/c/articles/6981526/continuous-time-red-black-how-control-diffusion-goal) **Direct implication for you:** because your failures are timeouts in weak regimes, your operative objective is to minimize expected time-to-$1,250 in those regimes — which means protecting μ (expected $/day), not just shrinking σ. The K200 result is the empirical signature of violating this: it improved σ (maxDD halved) but degraded μ/velocity (pass30 nearly halved).

**Sid Browne (1999, *Advances in Applied Probability* 31(2):551–577, "Reaching Goals by a Deadline").** For maximizing P(wealth reaches goal b by fixed time T) in a Black-Scholes market, the optimal value function is J(π,t) = Φ(Φ⁻¹(π/b) + ((μ−r)/σ)·√(T−t)), [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1311.2550) and the optimal risky dollar exposure has the structural form proportional to (1/(σ√(T−t)))·(φ(ν)/Φ(ν)) with ν = Φ⁻¹(π/b). **Two structural facts that should drive your controller:**
- **Time scaling ∝ 1/√(T−t):** exposure should be *small when much time remains* and *grow as the deadline approaches*. This is the formal basis for "sprint as day 30 nears if still short of target."
- **Gap scaling via the Mills ratio φ(ν)/Φ(ν):** exposure should be *large when far below goal* and *shrink toward zero as you approach the goal* (and once the goal is reached, park risk-free). This formalizes "sprint with cushion" (expand risk when you have a cushion and are still chasing target) and "consistency-lock / de-risk once the target is in hand."
- **Caveat:** Browne's exact policy diverges as t→T (the "all-or-nothing" digital-option feature; the optimal terminal wealth is b·1{S_T>λ}). [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1308.6465) This is *not directly implementable* and must be capped — which is exactly where your contract caps, daily-loss budget, and floor reserve enter as the binding constraints that tame the theoretical blow-up.

**Kulldorff (1993, *SIAM J. Control and Optimization* 31(1):52–69) and Heath (1993).** For favorable games with a *time limit*, the optimal strategy is provably *time-dependent*: small bets when much time remains, large bets when time is running short. [Umn](http://users.stat.umn.edu/~sudde001/personal_page/Goals.pdf) Browne (1999) is the continuous-time multi-asset generalization. Constant-Kelly is explicitly *not* optimal for finite-horizon goal-reaching because of overshoot near the goal. [umn](http://users.stat.umn.edu/~sudde001/personal_page/Goals.pdf) [Umn](http://users.stat.umn.edu/~sudde001/personal_page/Goals.pdf)

**Dubins & Savage (1965/1976, *How to Gamble If You Must*) — bold play.** In a *subfair* game (negative edge), bold play — stake min(x, g−x), i.e., bet the most that doesn't overshoot the goal [umn](http://users.stat.umn.edu/~sudde001/personal_page/Goals.pdf) — maximizes the probability of reaching a fixed goal, and remains optimal under a deadline (Dvoretzky). [umn](http://users.stat.umn.edu/~sudde001/personal_page/Goals.pdf) **The house-limit subtlety is a genuine warning for your contract caps:** whether capped-bold remains optimal depends discontinuously on the ratio of bet-limit to goal (Wilkins 1972: optimal if limit/goal = 1/n; Heath-Pruitt-Sudderth 1972 and Schweinsberg 2005: *not* optimal for other ratios). [umn](http://users.stat.umn.edu/~sudde001/personal_page/Goals.pdf) Translation: naive "max size when desperate" rules can be suboptimal; the size cap must be chosen relative to the remaining gap, not fixed.

**Drawdown-constrained optimization (Grossman & Zhou 1993, *Mathematical Finance* 3(3):241–276; Cvitanic & Karatzas 1995; Chekhlov-Uryasev-Zabarankin 2005, "Drawdown Measure in Portfolio Optimization," *Int. J. Theoretical & Applied Finance* 8(1):13–58).** Grossman-Zhou prove that under a constraint that wealth never fall below a fixed fraction of its running maximum, [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0378426606001312) optimal risky exposure is a *constant proportion of the gap between current wealth and the protected floor* — i.e., risk should scale with (equity − floor). This is the precise theoretical justification for making your per-trade risk budget a function of the floor gap F (the trailing-drawdown "underwater" distance), exactly the moving lower barrier in your challenge. Angoshtari-Bayraktar-Young (2016, "Optimal investment to minimize the probability of drawdown," *Stochastics* 88(6):946–958) further show the drawdown-minimizing policy is *identical* to the ruin-minimizing policy and is μ/σ²-maximizing, with optimal risky dollar exposure π*(w) = 2·(c(w)−rw)/(μ−r), independent of the running maximum, and → 0 as wealth approaches a "safe level" — i.e., de-risk hard once a cushion makes the floor unreachable. Chekhlov-Uryasev-Zabarankin's CDaR (Conditional Drawdown-at-Risk, the average of the worst (1−β) drawdowns, [Stevens](https://researchwith.stevens.edu/en/publications/portfolio-optimization-with-drawdown-constraints) LP-solvable) [Breakingdownfinance](https://breakingdownfinance.com/finance-topics/alternative-investments/conditional-drawdown-at-risk-cdar/) is the right *objective function* to optimize your controller against, because it targets the tail of the drawdown curve rather than a single worst point.

**Synthesis — the controller the theory prescribes:**

| State | Theory-prescribed posture | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Far below target, time abundant (e.g., day 3, P≈$0) | *Moderate* risk; do not over-bet early (Browne 1/√(T−t) says exposure is small when T−t large) | Browne; Kulldorff |
| Far below target, deadline near (day 22+, P<$600) | *Escalate* risk/velocity (sprint) — but capped by floor reserve and daily-loss budget | Browne; Kulldorff |
| Cushion built, still chasing target | Expand risk proportional to (equity − floor) | Grossman-Zhou |
| Target nearly reached / consistency-locked | De-risk toward zero; protect the floor | Browne Mills ratio; Angoshtari et al. |
| Weak regime detected | Maximize μ/σ² (protect drift; do not just cut size) | Pestien-Sudderth |
| Near the floor | Floor-defense: minimize P(drawdown) = μ/σ²-max with shrinking exposure | Angoshtari-Bayraktar-Young |

### B. Tail-loss control without over-filtering (the core fix)

Your pathology is concentration: top 5 losses = 58.1% of gross loss and ~88% of net profit; stops average −$134 while take-profits average +$49. The negative-skew literature ("picking up pennies in front of a steamroller," short-volatility profiles) is unanimous that this profile is dangerous, but also warns that *uniform stop-tightening kills the fat right tail you depend on*. So the fix is selective.

**1. MAE/MFE-based stop ceilings (John Sweeney, *Maximum Adverse Excursion*, Wiley 1996).** MAE is the worst intra-trade unrealized loss; plotting MAE against final P&L across ≥50–100 trades reveals the adverse-excursion level beyond which trades almost never recover. This is *empirical, data-driven* stop placement rather than structural guesswork. Grade: **practitioner-origin but methodologically sound and directly testable** on your 2 years of 1-minute data. The actionable design is a *volatility-scaled stop ceiling*: stop = min(structural_stop, k·ATR, MAE-derived cap by symbol/setup). Apply the cap hardest to the MNQ "base" setup that produces your −$512/−$256/−$209 outliers.

**2. ATR/volatility-scaled vs fixed vs structural stops.** The evidence base here is mostly practitioner; treat numeric claims skeptically. The defensible principle is that a *ceiling* expressed in ATR units adapts to regime (wider in volatile regimes, tighter in calm), which prevents a single high-volatility late-morning move from producing a −$512 loss while not over-tightening in normal conditions. Validate per-symbol because MNQ and MES have very different tick economics.

**3. A dedicated LOSS-TAIL model, not a win/lose classifier.** Your own AUCs are the key insight: fast-target AUC≈0.81 and transaction-completion≈0.71 are predictive, while the win head≈0.59 is near-random. This matches the theory: you cannot predict *whether* you win, but you *can* predict where the slow failures and tails live. Build heads that predict loss_tail probability, time-to-stop vs time-to-target, and expected MAE/MFE, and route only high-predicted-tail trades to scratch-before-stop or size-down — preserving density (your pass30 correlates ~0.53 with density, ~0.50 with avg PnL, only ~0.09 with win rate).

**4. Time-of-day / event throttle on the 11:00–12:40 ET MNQ window.** Your largest losers cluster in late morning. The literature supports two mechanisms: (a) the **lunch-hour liquidity lull** — the U-shaped intraday volume/volatility pattern (Biais et al. 1995; documented across futures) means midday has thin liquidity, wider effective spreads, and choppy reversals, which is hostile to a negative-skew scalper; and (b) **scheduled macro/monetary events** — the strongest *peer-reviewed* effect is the **pre-FOMC announcement drift** (Lucca & Moench, NY Fed Staff Report 512 / 2015 *Journal of Finance*): the S&P 500 "has on average increased 49 basis points in the 24 hours prior to the FOMC announcements," and "excess returns on U.S. stocks have on average been more than 30 times larger on announcement days than on other days," with roughly 80% of the post-1994 annual equity premium earned in that pre-announcement window. Critically, Lucca-Moench find this is *specific to FOMC*: "Other major U.S. macroeconomic news announcements also do not give rise to pre-announcement excess equity returns." **Actionable, evidence-supported throttle:** size down or wide-stop-skip in the 11:00–12:40 window (liquidity lull) and on FOMC days specifically; treat generic 10:00/10:30 ET data releases (ISM, EIA) as volatility-burst risk to *manage* (wider effective spreads) rather than as a documented return anomaly. Grade: liquidity U-shape is **peer-reviewed**; FOMC-day effect is **peer-reviewed and FOMC-specific**; the specific midday "lunch reversal" is **anecdotal/practitioner**. This is the most concrete, defensible single change targeting your documented loss cluster.

### C. Profit velocity under the $450 daily cap (convert 31–60-day passes into ≤30)

The $450/day cap plus consistency rule means you cannot solve a slow regime by hitting one huge day — you must accumulate. Dubins-Savage/Kulldorff say: when you're behind and time is short, escalate; but the cap bounds the escalation. The theory and your data jointly imply the lever is **more density and higher average win when a cushion exists**, not bigger single bets:
- Your pass30 correlates ~0.53 with trade density and ~0.50 with avg PnL but only ~0.09 with win rate. So adding *quality* trades (density) and lifting avg win (partial-profit runners) move the needle; raising accuracy does not.
- Because the cap truncates upside per day, the optimal pacing is closer to "consistent ~$250–$400 days" than "one $450 day." Goal-reaching theory (minimize expected time to G) plus the cap implies you want the *highest sustainable per-day drift*, which argues for the momentum sleeve (Section E) to add per-day expected profit in weak regimes for the scalper.
- "Stop trading when daily goal hit" rules: there is practitioner support but weak evidence. Under the consistency rule, deliberately *capping good days near the consistency ceiling* (≈40% of running total) is actually rational because an unusually large day raises the effective target. This is a genuine, under-appreciated optimization: once a day approaches its consistency-safe maximum, stop adding risk that day.

### D. Position sizing for negative skew under a ruin constraint

With payoff ratio ≈0.37 (avg win/avg loss = $52/$142) and WR 81.8%, the discrete Kelly fraction f* = p − (1−p)/b = 0.818 − 0.182/0.368 ≈ 0.32 of bankroll-at-risk per trade — which is *absurd* to apply literally given (a) estimation error, (b) non-stationarity (your regime cliff), and (c) negative skew/fat left tail that the Kelly binary model ignores. The Kelly literature (Thorp; MacLean-Thorp-Ziemba, *The Kelly Capital Growth Investment Criterion*) is explicit: overbetting is far more destructive than underbetting. MacLean, Ziemba & Blazenko (1992, *Management Science* 38:1562–85) quantify the fractional-Kelly tradeoff: half-Kelly "provides approximately 75% of the growth rate of Full Kelly but with only 50% of the volatility," and gives "a 99% chance of not losing more than half the wealth versus only 91.6% for [full] Kelly." Under estimation error and fat tails, quarter-Kelly or less is standard. **For you: heavy fractional/ capped sizing is mandatory**, and the right per-trade risk should be the *minimum* of (fractional-Kelly cap, remaining daily-loss budget, floor_gap − reserve, consistency headroom, contract cap) — i.e., the binding constraint is almost always one of the challenge limits, not Kelly. The role of Kelly here is only to set an *upper bound* you should stay well under, and to confirm that the floor-gap-proportional sizing (Grossman-Zhou) is the operative rule.

### E. Complementary positive-skew sleeve

Adding a positive-skew momentum sleeve mechanically offsets the small-win/large-loss scalper's distribution and can reach the target faster in the weak regimes where the scalper times out. The strongest *peer-reviewed* anchor is **Baltussen, Da, Lammers & Martens (2021, *Journal of Financial Economics* 142:377–403, "Hedging Demand and Market Intraday Momentum")**: across 60+ futures over 1974–2020, the last-30-minutes return is positively and significantly predicted by the rest-of-day return, mechanistically linked to gamma-hedging demand from option market-makers and leveraged ETFs, with asset-class-level annualized Sharpe ratios between 0.87 and 1.73. This is a tradeable, economically-grounded, end-of-day intraday momentum signal in the exact instruments you trade (ES/NQ).

Grade honestly: the **Zarattini-Barbon-Aziz ORB papers** report eye-catching numbers but are **practitioner/commercial with idealized assumptions**. Their 2024 "A Profitable Day Trading Strategy for the U.S. Equity Market" (Swiss Finance Institute Research Paper No. 24-98) reports a top-20-Stocks-in-Play portfolio with "a total net performance of over 1,600% [1,637%], with a Sharpe ratio of 2.81, and an annualized alpha of 36%" vs the S&P 500's 198% over Jan 2016–Dec 2023. Their earlier "Can Day Trading Really Be Profitable?" (2023) reports a TQQQ variant growing "$25,000... to $6,400,000 (net of commissions)" (+9,350%) — but the authors themselves concede "this result can under certain circumstances be considered unrealistic as our model assumed no slippage" (the more conservative QQQ base case returned 675%, Sharpe ~1). Backtests assume no bid-ask spread, 4x leverage, recorded prices at exact times, and equities not futures; independent replications found results *highly sensitive to data provider and parameters*. Do not treat their numbers as established. Use ORB only as a *structural template* (opening-range or anchored-VWAP trend), validated on your own futures data with realistic fills. The fact that your momentum-exhaustion hybrid already lifted pass60 to 76.9% and cut timeouts 17/39→9/39 is the in-sample confirmation that a path-shaping, skew-complementary event helps; the next step is sizing the sleeve so it adds drift in weak regimes (converting 31–60-day passes to ≤30) without adding left-tail risk.

### F. Additive ML ideas (push past the risk-head plan)

- **Quantile regression / quantile (pinball) loss** for MAE/MFE and time-to-stop: directly models the conditional *tail* of adverse excursion, the quantity you need for stop ceilings and scratch decisions. **Well-supported.**
- **Conformal prediction / conformalized quantile regression** (Vovk et al. 2005; Angelopoulos & Bates 2021, "A Gentle Introduction to Conformal Prediction"): gives distribution-free, finite-sample coverage guarantees for prediction intervals under exchangeability, enabling principled *abstention* — skip or size-down a trade when the predicted loss interval is too wide. The exchangeability assumption [Sage Journals](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/25152459251380452) is violated by your regime non-stationarity, so use block/time-series conformal variants (Chernozhukov et al. 2018) and treat coverage as approximate. **Well-supported but with a stated caveat.**
- **Calibration (isotonic/Platt, reliability diagrams, ECE)**: a *prerequisite* before any probability drives sizing — uncalibrated p_stop or loss_tail_p will systematically mis-size. **Well-supported, low-risk, do this first.**
- **Cost-sensitive / asymmetric loss** that penalizes large-loss false negatives more than missed wins: aligns the model with your asymmetric payoff. **Well-supported.**
- **Quickest change detection (CUSUM — Page 1954; Shiryaev-Roberts; Moustakides; Tartakovsky-Veeravalli)** for the regime cliff: CUSUM is minimax-optimal (Lorden) for detecting a distributional change with minimum delay at a controlled false-alarm rate, and Shiryaev-Roberts is optimal for changes at a distant horizon. Real-time financial-surveillance applications exist (Pepelyshev-Polunchenko). Use it to detect the Feb-2026-type regime shift early and switch the controller to floor-defense/conservative mode. **Well-supported and directly relevant to your regime-concentrated failures.**
- **Distributional RL with CVaR/drawdown objectives** (IQN-based actor-critic; risk-sensitive policies): conceptually ideal for the sizing/pacing controller because it optimizes the *full return distribution* and can target the left tail directly. But it is **reproducibility-fragile, sample-hungry, and prone to overfitting non-stationary intraday data** — keep it last, behind gradient-boosting risk heads, and only after the simpler pieces validate. **Promising but speculative for your data regime.**

### G. Selection-bias-robust validation (extend your existing battery)

You already use split-half, month-start sensitivity, walk-forward with embargo, and ≥10k-path block-bootstrap Monte Carlo. Add:
- **Deflated Sharpe Ratio (Bailey & Lopez de Prado 2014, *Journal of Portfolio Management* 40(5):94–107)** and **Probability of Backtest Overfitting / CSCV (Bailey, Borwein, Lopez de Prado, Zhu 2017)**: you are selecting the best pass30 from dozens of candidates across ~39 starts with a hard regime split — the textbook setup for an inflated winner. DSR adjusts the significance of a result for the number of trials, plus skewness and kurtosis (critical given your negative skew); PBO estimates the probability the selected configuration is overfit. **A ~5-point pass30 improvement should be tested against the distribution of best-of-N pass30 under the null** before it is believed.
- **Combinatorial Purged Cross-Validation (CPCV, Lopez de Prado, *Advances in Financial Machine Learning*)**: generates many train/test splits with purging and embargo, giving a *distribution* of out-of-sample pass rates rather than a single path — far more robust than one walk-forward.
- **Regime-aware block bootstrap, NOT day-shuffle.** Day-shuffle Monte Carlo destroys the serial/regime clustering that *is* your failure mechanism (Feb-onward timeouts), so it will be systematically over-optimistic about pass30. Bootstrap *blocks* (weeks/regimes) to preserve the clustering, and report pass30/pass60 conditional on regime.

Prioritized, each with a success threshold and a kill criterion. Validate every step with leakage-free OOS, slippage stress (slip3), and the harsh NinjaTrader fill model *before* risking capital — recall that fill realism alone takes control pass30/pass60 from 30.8%/56.4% to ~10.3%/12.8%, so any improvement must survive that gauntlet.

2. Time-of-day/event throttle: size-down or wide-stop-skip in 11:00–12:40 ET (liquidity lull) and on FOMC days specifically; manage 10:00/10:30 ET data-release volatility bursts as execution risk. *Success:* the top-5-loss share of gross loss falls below ~45% (from 58.1%) without cutting pass30. *Kill:* pass30 declines, or the throttle removes >15% of trades (too blunt).

**Stage 2 — State-dependent controller (the theory in code).**
3. Implement the floor-gap-proportional, time-scaled risk budget: per-trade risk = min(fractional-Kelly cap [≤¼ of f*≈0.32, i.e., ≤~8% notional risk proxy], remaining daily-loss budget, floor_gap − reserve, consistency headroom, contract cap), with the *base* risk rising as days-remaining shrinks while still below target (Browne 1/√(T−t)) and expanding with the cushion (Grossman-Zhou). *Success:* pass30 ≥ 36% and pass60 ≥ 70% with maxDD ≤ ~$700 (near your hybrid's $686). *Kill:* maxDD breaches toward bust risk (floor actually threatened) or pass30 < 33%.
4. Add CUSUM/Shiryaev-Roberts regime detector that switches the controller to floor-defense/conservative when a weak-regime change is flagged. *Success:* reduces timeouts in Feb-onward starts (target: ≤9/39, matching hybrid). *Kill:* false alarms cut density and lower pass30.

**Stage 3 — Positive-skew sleeve.**
5. Add a small, independently-sized end-of-day intraday-momentum sleeve (Baltussen et al. structure) validated on your own ES/NQ data with realistic fills. *Success:* combined pass30 → ~38–40% and pass60 → ~78%+ with timeouts < 9/39, *and* the blend's skew is less negative than the scalper alone. *Kill:* the sleeve's standalone OOS edge is indistinguishable from zero after costs, or it raises combined maxDD.

**Stage 4 — ML refinement (last).**
6. Calibrate existing heads (isotonic), add quantile-loss MAE/MFE heads and conformal abstention; only then consider distributional/CVaR RL for the controller. *Success:* calibrated loss_tail_p measurably improves the scratch-decision precision (fewer large losses at equal density). *Kill:* any ML layer fails CPCV/DSR/PBO — revert to the rules-based controller.

**Validation gates (apply at every stage):** A change is "real" only if (a) its pass30 lift survives the best-of-N null distribution (DSR/PBO), (b) it holds under regime-aware block bootstrap (not day-shuffle), and (c) it survives slip3 + NinjaTrader fills. If any gate fails, do not deploy.

## Caveats
- **No design guarantees a 90% pass rate, or anything close.** Intraday equity-index signal-to-noise is low; your win head is near-random (AUC≈0.59); and your edge is regime-fragile. Realistic fills alone roughly *halve-to-third* your pass rates. Treat any single-config pass30 above ~40% with suspicion until it clears DSR/PBO.
- **The goal-reaching theory is a Black-Scholes/diffusion idealization.** Browne's exact policy diverges near the deadline and assumes continuous trading, constant coefficients, and frictionless markets — none true for you. Use it for *policy structure* (when to be aggressive vs conservative), not literal position sizes; your challenge caps are what make it implementable.
- **Practitioner sources are flagged and must not be taken at face value:** MAE/MFE (Sweeney), R-multiples/expectancy (Van Tharp), partial-profit "rules," ATR-stop claims, prop-firm marketing, and the Zarattini ORB numbers are practitioner/commercial, often with idealized fills, leverage, and cherry-picked windows. The peer-reviewed anchors are Browne, Pestien-Sudderth, Dubins-Savage, Kulldorff, Grossman-Zhou, Cvitanic-Karatzas, Chekhlov-Uryasev-Zabarankin, Angoshtari-Bayraktar-Young, Baltussen et al., Lucca-Moench, MacLean-Ziemba-Blazenko, Bailey-Lopez de Prado, Angelopoulos-Bates, and the CUSUM/quickest-detection literature (Page, Shiryaev, Moustakides, Tartakovsky-Veeravalli).
- **Overfitting is the central risk.** You are mining a small number of regime-split starts; the more you tune, the more you fit noise. Every "improvement" is a hypothesis until it survives leakage-free, multiple-testing-corrected, regime-aware, fill-realistic validation.
- **This is research/education for your own informed system-development decisions, not financial advice.**
